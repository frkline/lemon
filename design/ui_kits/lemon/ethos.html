Design System · Foundations

The Lemon ethos

Lemon hangs off the menubar and runs coding agents against your Linear issues and GitHub repos. It is chrome that sits over your real desktop, so it has to feel like part of the machine — warm, dense, and quiet, until the one moment it isn’t. Seven principles hold the system together.

01
Material over stroke
Depth comes from the glass and its blur, not from borders. A surface steps forward by thickening its material — never by drawing a heavier line. Hairlines are half a pixel and barely there.
02
Warm, not cold
Every neutral is pulled toward amber and citrus. The chrome is dark, but it’s a dark that belongs beside a lemon — never gunmetal, never a cold gray panel.
03
Color is earned
Yellow is reserved for the single primary action. State lives in one small dot. Most of the interface is quiet warm-neutral text, because saturation is a signal and signals must stay scarce.
04
Machine truth in mono
Identifiers, paths, and console output are set in SF Mono — the literal truth handed back by Linear, GitHub, and the agent. Prose and labels are SF Pro. The typeface says what’s human and what’s machine.
05
Width-stable identity
Source marks are fixed-size favicon tiles. A list of sessions never reflows when a status flips, so the eye can rest on stable columns and scan down without jitter.
06
Quiet by default, loud when it matters
A resting row whispers. A live agent or a failure is the only thing allowed to raise its voice — and even then the loud signal is small, just unmistakable.
07
Built for the corner
Everything assumes a 340-pt popover in the top-right corner of the screen. Density is high, hit targets stay at least 28 pt, and nothing presumes a full window to breathe in.
—
In one line
Liquid Glass, meticulous and Apple-quiet: warm dark, mono where it counts, color only when something is actually happening.
